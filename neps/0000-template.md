## Summary

## Motivation

What problem does this solve, and for whom? Show the program that is awkward
today, and say exactly what is awkward about it. A proposal without a concrete
example is a preference, and preferences belong in a discussion.


## Guide-level explanation

Explain the change as if to someone who will use it: what they write, what it
does, what it refuses to do. Keep it to the surface a user sees.

## Reference-level explanation

The details an implementer needs: rules, interactions with existing features,
and the error cases with the `NDO` codes they produce.

## What this makes impossible

forbids, and say why that is acceptable. If you cannot name one, you have not
looked.

## Open questions

Things this proposal does not settle. Each one is an invitation to object, and
an accepted NEP must have none left open that affect its own rules.

## Prior art

What other languages or systems do, and what happened to them. Not required to
be exhaustive, but a proposal that ignores prior art usually re-invents a known
mistake.

## Unresolved objections

Filled in during review. An objection is unresolved until it is answered on the
record or the design changes because of it.
